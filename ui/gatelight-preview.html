    Steel and ink, with four decision colors

    Chrome, cards and tables never carry color. Verdigris, Garnet, Saffron and Lapis appear only for a permit, a denial, something pending, or a cryptographic fact. Every status also changes its glyph and its word, so color is never the only signal.

    PermittedGate A and Gate B both passed. The aperture is open.
    DeniedOutside clearance or scope. The aperture is closed.
    Pending, 8 min leftA just-in-time grant waiting for approval or about to expire.
    VerifiedA signature, hash, or exact quote was checked. Always set in Lapis.
    One accent for brand momentsBeam is the pale cyan dot inside the aperture. It is the logo, the loader, and the glint on sealed surfaces, never a button color.
    Hairlines, not shadowsCards lift with a 1px ring and a faint inner highlight. Real shadows are reserved for drawers and dialogs.
    Modality is a quiet tintDocument, image, audio, video and code each get a low-chroma tint. The glyph carries identity, so these never compete with the four decision colors.

    Gatelight glyphs replace all 81 icons and the four emoji

    Each glyph is built from circles and quarter-arcs with one deliberate gap, the gate opening, and a single beam element in the accent color. Click any glyph to copy its SVG.

    Clearance you can see

    Classification levels are five stacked bars. Your clearance lights the bars from the bottom; a compartment's ceiling reaches higher. The hatched gap is exactly what a just-in-time request would unlock.

    Depth dial

    Retrieval depth stops being green, blue and purple. One, two or three rings mean a deeper pipeline. The thumb slides on a spring.

    The session ring

    The five-minute inactivity timer becomes a ring that drains. It turns Saffron under a minute and starts breathing at thirty seconds. Renewing refills it clockwise.

    A ledger you can watch being verified

    Each audit event links to the one before it by hash. Verification sweeps down the chain and seals each link. Tamper with a block and the sweep stops at the break.

    Fingerprints are drawn from each hash, so two different blocks never look alike.

    Federation as a topology

    Motion that explains

    Arrivals ease out, morphs ease in and out, and segmented controls use a spring. Fade-and-slide entrances are not applied to every card; motion is reserved for answering an action or showing a decision.
